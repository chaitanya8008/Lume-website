Guide · Lighting

Cool, natural or warm — which mirror light do you actually need?

Light colour changes everything about how a mirror works: how your skin looks, how detail reads, how the room feels. Here's the honest breakdown — and why we stopped asking customers to choose.

The three temperatures, in plain words

Cool white · 6000K

Crisp, bright, slightly blue. This is task light — it maximises contrast, so it's the best setting for shaving, eyeliner, threading eyebrows, checking for stray hairs. It can make warm skin tones look a touch pale, which is exactly why you shouldn't judge your final makeup under it.

Natural white · 4500K

The neutral middle — closest to indirect daylight. Colours read truest here, which makes it the reference setting for makeup that will be seen outdoors or in an office. If you could only have one temperature, this is the one to have. (With a Lume mirror you don't have to pick — more on that below.)

Warm white · 3000K

Golden and soft, like evening lamp light. This is ambience light — flattering on all skin tones, gentle on tired eyes, and the setting that makes a bathroom feel like a hotel at 10 pm. It hides fine detail, so switch out of it for close-up work.

Matching light to the room

  • Bathroom vanity — you genuinely need all three: cool for grooming, natural for makeup, warm for the evening wind-down.
  • Bedroom dressing mirror — natural white as the default; warm white if the room leans wood, brass or terracotta.
  • Powder room — warm white flatters guests; nobody does precision grooming in a powder room.
  • Salon or studio — natural white, always. Clients decide with daylight eyes.

Why Lume mirrors are 3-in-1 as standard

Every Lume mirror ships with all three colours in one strip. Tap the touch sensor to cycle cool → natural → warm; press and hold to dim; the mirror remembers your last brightness when it switches back on. You can still order a single fixed colour if a project spec demands it — but for a home, switchable costs you nothing and settles every argument at the basin.

One placement tip that matters more than temperature

Light that reaches your face from the front (frosted patterns on the mirror face, or a lit border) is what removes shadows under the eyes and chin. A purely backlit halo mirror looks spectacular on the wall but lights the wall, not your face. Many of our designs combine both — a face-lit pattern plus a halo glow.
